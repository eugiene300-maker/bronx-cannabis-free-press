Bronx Weed Prices, August 2026: Legal Prices Keep Sliding as Supply Grows

New York's legal market sold $895.4 million in the first half of 2026 as per-unit prices fell and supply surged. Here is what that means at a Bronx counter, and how to stack the savings.

Key takeaways

  • Per-unit legal cannabis prices in New York fell about 7 percent from January to June 2026, according to the Office of Cannabis Management's July data.
  • Statewide legal sales reached $895.4 million in the first half of 2026, and the state forecasts $2.5 billion to $3 billion for the full year.
  • Licensed canopy grew about 32 percent since March, and New York had 683 adult-use dispensaries by June, pushing prices down.
  • With 13 percent tax, a basket that cost $113 out the door in January cost about $105 by June at the same shelf mix.
  • First-time deals, loyalty points, daily specials and group discounts are the most reliable ways to save.

Legal weed in New York keeps getting cheaper, and New Yorkers keep buying it. Per-unit cannabis prices fell about 7 percent statewide from January to June 2026, according to data the Office of Cannabis Management presented to the Cannabis Control Board on July 2. Over the same six months, licensed stores sold $895.4 million worth of product.

Lower prices, more stores and a lot more supply are all pulling in the same direction. The state now expects the legal market to finish 2026 somewhere between $2.5 billion and $3 billion in sales. Here is what the numbers say, and how to use them the next time you are at the counter on Fordham Road or Third Avenue.

The pattern is clear: products are getting cheaper while the market keeps growing. A rough back-of-envelope calculation, dividing first-half sales by six, puts the legal market at around $149 million a month on average. To hit even the low end of the state's forecast, the second half of the year would need to run well ahead of that pace, which means more shoppers, more items per trip, or both.

What the data does not show

One caveat before you take these numbers to the counter: they are statewide. There is no official Bronx-only price series, so there is no state figure for what an eighth costs on Fordham Road versus Third Avenue. The per-unit price also blends everything from a single pre-roll to a vape cart to a multi-pack of gummies. Treat it as a direction of travel, not a price tag. What the statewide trend does tell Bronx shoppers is that the pressure on prices is broad, and it is not letting up.

Why prices are falling

A lot more weed is being grown

Licensed cultivation canopy in New York has grown sharply this year, with more licensed growers bringing more acreage online. More supply chasing the same customers is the oldest pricing story there is.

More stores are competing for you

The state counted 683 adult-use dispensaries as of June, and its 600th store opened March 31 on the Upper West Side, according to the Office of Cannabis Management's July 2 presentation. The Cannabis Control Board keeps approving new licenses at its monthly meetings, and every new storefront is one more menu competing on price. In the Bronx alone, four licensed stores have opened so far this year, most recently Upscale Cannabis in Pelham Gardens on Aug. 7.

Operators are feeling it

Not everyone is celebrating. At the Cannabis Control Board's August meeting, operators described sales as sharply declining, and the state gave stores a grace period on their community impact plans until their first license renewal. Per the July data, the median New York store brings in about $2.6 million a year, while the average is $3.5 million, a gap that suggests a few big earners are pulling the average up.

Cheaper weed is good news at the register, but a price war fought on thin margins can squeeze the small equity shops the Bronx fought to get.

What it means for Bronx shoppers

The register math

Shelf prices in New York already include a THC-based wholesale tax. At checkout you add 13 percent more: 9 percent state plus 4 percent local. To see what a 7 percent drop means out the door, take a basket that rang up at $100 in shelf prices in January and buy the same mix in June:

January 2026June 2026
Shelf price, same basket$100.00$93.00
Plus 13% state and local tax$13.00$12.09
Out-the-door estimate$113.00$105.09

That is about $7.90 saved on every $100 of product, before any discount, and the tax savings compound the shelf savings because the 13 percent is charged on a smaller number. These are statewide averages across all product types, not Bronx shelf prices, so your receipt will vary by store and category.

Cheaper is not weaker

Lower prices make it tempting to trade up to vapes and concentrates. If you do, remember the legal public possession limit for concentrate is 24 grams, and potency is much higher than flower. A cheaper gram is not a smaller dose. Start low.

Bronx competition is local

The Bronx has 23 licensed stores, and in a few spots they are close enough to compete head-to-head. Arthur Avenue has Freshly Baked and Guardian Wellness on the same strip. On East Tremont Avenue in Throggs Neck, Say Less and Victory Dispensary operate a few blocks apart, a pairing we covered in our Throggs Neck report. Third Avenue and Boston Road each have two stores as well. Where stores cluster, shoppers can compare menus before they walk in. Find yours on the Bronx dispensary map.

How to actually pay less

You cannot control wholesale markets, but you can control how you shop. The most reliable savings are the ones stores publish:

  1. Use the first-time deal. Ask for one at any new store. Buddiez Bronx Cannabis Dispensary, which sponsors the Free Press, for example, offers a first-time customer discount.
  2. Join a loyalty program. Points programs reward repeat visits, and they add up if you shop at one or two stores regularly.
  3. Check daily specials before you leave home. Many stores post specials online. If you are in the South Bronx, you can check current dispensary deals in the Bronx at Buddiez before heading out; compare other stores' specials pages the same way.
  4. Ask about group discounts. Some stores offer veteran, union or other discounts. Buddiez, for instance, offers veteran and UFCW union discounts.
  5. Order ahead. Online menus let you compare prices across stores without a trip. If you are ordering delivery, factor in minimums; our Bronx weed delivery guide lists which stores deliver.
The Free Press Take

Falling prices are real, but do not let them lure you to the unlicensed shop down the block. Prices far below legal stores are a red flag, not a bargain, because that product has not been lab-tested. The legal price gap is shrinking on its own. Always buy from a licensed store, bring a 21+ photo ID and never drive high.

What to watch this fall

The hemp crackdown

A 2025 federal law caps hemp products at 0.4 milligrams of total THC per container and bans synthetic cannabinoids, starting in November 2026, the Office of Cannabis Management has told businesses. That will pull many intoxicating hemp products off bodega and smoke shop shelves. Whether it pushes more shoppers into licensed stores, and how that affects prices, is worth watching. Our hemp ban explainer covers what disappears and what stays.

New supply keeps coming

With growing capacity expanding and new licenses still being approved, the forces pushing prices down are not going away soon. The state's own forecast of $2.5 billion to $3 billion in 2026 sales assumes the market keeps growing even as prices fall. For the long view on how the Bronx fits into that market, see our five-year Bronx cannabis scorecard.

Bottom line: legal weed is cheaper than it was at the start of the year, and the smartest Bronx shoppers are stacking published deals on top of that trend. Just keep it legal, keep it at home and, with edibles, start low and go slow.

Frequently asked questions

Are weed prices going down in the Bronx?

Statewide data from the Office of Cannabis Management shows per-unit prices fell about 7 percent from January to June 2026. Bronx shelf prices vary by store and product, but the statewide trend is downward.

How much tax do you pay on legal weed in New York?

Shoppers pay 13 percent at checkout, 9 percent state plus 4 percent local. A THC-based wholesale tax is already built into the shelf price.

Why are cannabis prices falling in New York?

Supply has surged, with licensed canopy up about 32 percent since March, and New York had 683 adult-use dispensaries competing for customers by June. The state's own data showed per-unit prices down about 7 percent from January to June.

What is the best way to save at a Bronx dispensary?

Use first-time customer deals, join loyalty programs, check each store's specials page before you go and ask about veteran or union discounts.
