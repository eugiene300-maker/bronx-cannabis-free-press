Word on the Block: The Five Bronx Dispensary Debates of Summer 2026

Crowded corridors, cut padlocks, a new sheriff, falling prices and schoolyard worries. Here's what the record says.

Key takeaways

  • CB10 backed Victory Dispensary in Throggs Neck by just 13–10, versus 29–0 for a Williamsbridge Road applicant.
  • Indica Diamond allegedly cut a city padlock; 800+ pounds were seized. It was never licensed.
  • Mayor Mamdani replaced Sheriff Miranda with Edwin Raymond; federal agents later searched Miranda's home.
  • State data show per-unit legal cannabis prices fell about 7% from January to June 2026.
  • A federal hemp THC cap takes effect in November, targeting bodega and smoke-shop products.

Summer 2026 was loud for Bronx cannabis. A padlock got cut in Pelham Gardens. A Mott Haven shop got raided for the third time. The city got a new sheriff, and the old one got a visit from federal agents. Prices kept sliding, and store owners told state regulators the pressure was real. Meanwhile, residents packed community board meetings to argue about who gets to open where.

Here's our end-of-summer read on the five conversations that kept coming back, what the record actually says, and what we'll be watching this fall.

How We Read the Room

What we read

Our reporting rests on the public record: Community Board minutes, City Hall announcements, court rulings, Sheriff's Office enforcement actions, state Office of Cannabis Management data and Cannabis Control Board documents. Every number below comes from that record.

What we don't do

We also follow neighborhood Facebook groups, Reddit and X. We summarize the recurring themes there in broad strokes only. We don't quote users, name accounts or treat a viral post as a fact. Social chatter tells us what people are asking; the record tells us what's true.

1. Too Many Shops on One Block?

The story. Throggs Neck became the borough's test case. Victory Dispensary at 3653 E Tremont Ave opened May 5, 2026, state licensing records show, on the same stretch as Say Less at 3764 E Tremont Ave, which opened in December. Neighbors voiced strong opposition in the weeks before it opened. The owners, Francisco Gomez and Ali Zindani, promised armed security, ID scanning and 10 to 15 local hires.

The numbers. Community Board 10 backed Victory by just 13–10. Compare that to its 29–0 vote on April 16, 2026, recommending "I Bud You NY Inc." at 1446 Williamsbridge Rd, the board's minutes show. Borough-wide, the Bronx has 23 stores against Manhattan's 96 and Brooklyn's 82.

Our read. Clustering is a real complaint on specific corridors, but the Bronx as a whole is under-stored, not over-stored. The fight is about where, not whether.

What to watch. Licensed stores not yet open, including Monarch NYC, Green Heights and Thurston Hall. More in our Throggs Neck report.

2. Padlocked Shops Coming Back

The story. In June, Indica Diamond at 1334 E Gun Hill Rd, previously sealed by city order, allegedly cut its padlock and resumed sales. The Sheriff's Office seized more than 800 pounds, including 364 pounds of flavored vapes, and resealed it. Though it has sometimes been described as a dispensary, it has never held a state license. In May, Bud-Zotic at 516 Timpson Place was raided a third time, with 500-plus pounds taken from the store and 300-plus from a van, less than a year after an August 2025 raid there netted $3.2 million in product, including 1,100 pounds of flower.

The numbers. The Bronx logged 199 Operation Padlock closures by Aug. 13, 2024, barely three months into the program. Statewide, the Office of Cannabis Management reports about 500 sealing orders and $125 million in product seized.

Our read. A padlock is a pause, not an ending, and repeat offenders are the problem residents notice most. See our Indica Diamond coverage.

What to watch. Whether repeat-location penalties get tougher under the new sheriff.

3. Is Enforcement Fair?

The story. On May 29, Mayor Zohran Mamdani replaced Sheriff Anthony Miranda, the face of Operation Padlock and the subject of a DOI probe into cash seizures during raids (he denied wrongdoing), with Edwin Raymond, a retired NYPD lieutenant and whistleblower. On July 16, federal agents searched Miranda's Queens home; no arrests or charges have been disclosed. Raymond was sworn in late July.

The numbers. The courts have split: a Queens judge ordered one padlock lifted on due-process grounds in October 2024, while a federal court upheld closure of 27 shops in March 2025.

Our read. City Hall's announcement lists unlicensed-cannabis enforcement among Raymond's duties but doesn't name Padlock. That silence is the story. Full context in our new sheriff explainer.

What to watch. Any rebrand, any change in how cash is handled, and any movement in the federal inquiry.

Social chatter tells us what people are asking; the record tells us what's true.

4. Falling Prices, Struggling Owners

The story. Good news for your wallet, harder news for small shops. The Office of Cannabis Management's July data show per-unit prices statewide fell about 7% from January to June, even as legal sales for the first half of the year reached $895.4 million. At the Cannabis Control Board's August meeting, operators told the board their sales were dropping sharply; the state gave stores grace on community impact plans until their first renewal.

The numbers. Licensed growing capacity keeps expanding, which means more supply chasing the same customers. The state counted 683 adult-use dispensaries as of June, with median store revenue around $2.6 million a year.

Our read. More supply plus more stores means lower shelf prices. Equity owners in the Bronx, who make up 74% of the borough's stores, feel that squeeze first. Details in our August price report.

What to watch. Deals, loyalty programs and, unfortunately, possible closures.

5. Schools and Youth

The story. Hush in Allerton, across from P.S. 89, faced a closure fight in 2025 over the school-distance rule. On Feb. 12, 2026, Gov. Hochul signed a fix grandfathering 150-plus stores approved under mistaken measurements.

The numbers. On July 30, Community Connections for Youth's PODER Youth Leadership Collective graduated 13 South Bronx youth, funded by state cannabis community reinvestment grants ($5 million in round one, $15 million in round two). And in November, a federal cap of 0.4 milligrams of THC per container is scheduled to take effect for hemp products, which should clear intoxicating hemp off many bodega and smoke-shop shelves.

Our read. Licensed stores check ID at the door. The youth-access worry belongs mostly with unlicensed sellers. See our hemp ban explainer.

What to watch. What actually disappears from corner-store shelves after November.

Quick Hits

Not sure a shop is legal? Check it with the state's Dispensary Location Verification tool and look for the OCM QR sticker by the door. A licensed Bronx weed dispensary such as Buddiez Bronx Cannabis Dispensary (a Free Press sponsor) holds a license you can look up: OCM-CAURD-25-000297. The borough's newest licensed store, Upscale Cannabis at 3042b Fenton Ave in Pelham Gardens, opened Aug. 7. Always 21+, and never smoke in parks or within 100 feet of a school entrance.

Frequently asked questions

How many licensed dispensaries are open in the Bronx?

Twenty-three, according to state licensing records: 17 CAURD equity stores and 6 standard retail stores. The newest, Upscale Cannabis in Pelham Gardens, opened Aug. 7.

Why do padlocked smoke shops reopen?

Some operators allegedly break seals and resume sales, as officials say happened at Indica Diamond in June. Violations can bring penalties up to $25,000, and the Sheriff resealed that shop.

Did you quote Reddit or Facebook posts?

No. We summarize recurring social media themes in general terms and rely on public records and official statements for facts.

Are dispensaries allowed near Bronx schools?

Siting rules limit distance from schools, and a February 2026 law grandfathered 150-plus stores approved under mistaken measurements. Smoking is banned within 100 feet of school entrances.
