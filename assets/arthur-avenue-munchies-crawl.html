The Arthur Avenue Munchies Crawl: A Bronx Cannabis Lover's Food Guide

Two licensed dispensaries, seven legendary food stops and one rule: shop Belmont, then take it all home.

Key takeaways

  • Arthur Avenue has two licensed dispensaries: Freshly Baked NYC at 2375 and Guardian Wellness at 2500.
  • Mike's Deli tops our crawl at 5/5 for take-home munchie value and proximity to a licensed store.
  • Ferragosto runs Sunday, Sept. 13, noon to 6 p.m., free, between East 187th Street and Crescent Avenue.
  • Consume at home: cannabis use is banned in NYC restaurants, bars, outdoor dining areas and parks.

Arthur Avenue has never needed a reason to eat. Bread comes out of ovens that have been firing for a century, the deli counters still slice to order, and on a Saturday the sidewalk moves at the speed of people carrying pastry boxes. Now Belmont's Little Italy is also one of the few Bronx strips with two state-licensed dispensaries within a short walk of each other, which makes it the most natural munchies crawl in the Bronx.

This is our guide to doing it right: where to buy legal, where to eat, how each stop scores on our Friendliness Index, and the one rule that matters most. You shop on Arthur Avenue. You consume at home.

How We Score It: The Friendliness Index

Every stop below gets a rating on our 1-to-5 leaf scale, written as "Friendliness: 4/5." It's an editorial score built from public information and the law, not staff visits, and it weighs five things:

  • Vibe: Is it relaxed, forgiving, and easy to navigate when you are planning your evening around snacks?
  • Late hours: Can you still get something after the dinner rush? Where hours aren't published, we don't guess.
  • Munchie value: Does it deliver big flavor, takeout that travels, or something you'll want at 11 p.m. on your couch?
  • Proximity to a licensed dispensary: How close is it to a store on the state's license list?
  • Legality of consuming nearby: Always the same answer here. No restaurant, bar, outdoor dining area or park allows cannabis use, so points go to places whose food travels well back to your apartment.

The full methodology lives on our Cannabis Friendliness Index page.

Start Legal: The Two Dispensaries on the Strip

State licensing records show both of Arthur Avenue's dispensaries are open and licensed. Before you hand anyone money, you can check any storefront against the state's Dispensary Location Verification tool and look for the OCM QR sticker near the entrance.

Freshly Baked NYC, 2375 Arthur Ave

Freshly Baked opened in May 2024 under a CAURD license, the equity license reserved for people with past cannabis convictions and their families. Owners David and Pamela Nicponski started out running a Queens delivery business before taking over the former Fabco Shoes space on the avenue.

Guardian Wellness, 2500 Arthur Ave

Up the avenue, Guardian Wellness opened March 1, 2025, with a standard adult-use retail license. It is women-owned, and the store lists delivery to the Bronx, Yonkers and Mount Vernon from noon to 8 p.m. daily. That's useful if you want to eat on Arthur Avenue and have the rest of your order meet you at home.

If your day starts in the South Bronx instead, the Bronx dispensary Buddiez Bronx Cannabis Dispensary at 2935 Third Ave, three blocks north of The Hub, is another licensed option (Buddiez sponsors the Free Press). For every open store in the borough, see our Bronx dispensary map.

Buy it on Arthur Avenue, carry it home with a bag of bread, and let the couch be your trattoria.

The Crawl, Stop by Stop

This route runs roughly from the Retail Market end of Arthur Avenue out to Crescent Avenue, with a detour onto East 187th Street. Hours vary by day and by season, so check each business before you go.

1. Madonia Brothers Bakery, 2348 Arthur Ave

Madonia Brothers has been baking on Arthur Avenue since 1918. Grab a loaf early and you've already solved the midnight question: bread, olive oil, done.

Friendliness: 4/5. Huge take-home munchie value and steps from Freshly Baked. Points off only because a bakery is a daytime play, not a late-night one.

2. Mike's Deli, 2344 Arthur Ave (Arthur Avenue Retail Market)

Inside the Arthur Avenue Retail Market, Mike's Deli is the counter people plan their whole trip around. A deli hero wrapped for the road is close to the perfect munchies purchase: it survives the train, it tastes better an hour later, and it feeds two.

Friendliness: 5/5. Classic vibe, top-tier munchie value, and practically next door to a licensed dispensary. The market is an indoor food hall, so the rule is simple: buy here, eat at home.

3. Bronx Beer Hall (Arthur Avenue Retail Market)

The Bronx Beer Hall shares the market space and is a genuinely fun hang. It's also a bar, which is where our score gets honest. Cannabis use is banned in bars, and mixing alcohol with cannabis tends to hit harder than either one alone.

Friendliness: 2/5. Great vibe, great location, low score for the purposes of this crawl. If you go, go before, not after, and don't drive.

4. Zero Otto Nove, 2357 Arthur Ave

Zero Otto Nove is named for 089, the telephone area code of Salerno, hometown of chef Roberto Paciullo. The restaurant describes its kitchen as serving "what they eat in Italy today, not what the immigrants brought." Think a sit-down Southern Italian meal and pizza with a crust built on dough, not gimmicks.

Friendliness: 3/5. Excellent food and steps from Freshly Baked, but this is a reservation-and-conversation kind of place. Best as the dinner you enjoy clear-headed, with leftovers for later.

5. Full Moon Pizza, 600 E 187th St

Just off the avenue on East 187th Street, Full Moon is the neighborhood slice-shop counterweight to the white-tablecloth spots. A whole pie reheats better than almost anything else in this guide.

Friendliness: 4/5. Easy vibe, strong take-home value, a short walk from both dispensaries. Eat it at home, not on a neighbor's stoop.

6. Artuso Pastry, 670 E 187th St

Artuso has been turning out Italian pastry on East 187th Street since 1946. A white box tied with string is the most civilized thing you can bring home to a living room full of friends, and pastries hold up for tomorrow morning, too.

Friendliness: 4/5. Sweet-tooth heaven and travel-friendly. Like the bakery, it's a daytime stop, so plan the crawl accordingly.

7. Ajo y Orégano, 621 Crescent Ave

Here's the plot twist on a Little Italy crawl: a Dominican restaurant with a "campo style" theme. The menu runs to mofongo and mofonguitos, pollo guisado, pernil, chicharrón de pollo and chinola juice. The Crescent Avenue location lists hours of 12:30 to 9 p.m. Monday through Thursday and until 10 p.m. Friday through Sunday, the latest published close on this route.

Friendliness: 4/5. Big, comforting plates that travel, the latest hours on the list, and a good reminder that the Bronx doesn't do single-cuisine neighborhoods.

Coming Up: Ferragosto Sunday, September 13

If you want the avenue at full volume, circle this date. Bronx Little Italy's Ferragosto festival runs Sunday, September 13, from noon to 6 p.m., rain or shine, on Arthur Avenue between East 187th Street and Crescent Avenue. Admission is free; food, drinks and merchandise are for sale. It's a tradition of more than 25 years that draws thousands.

A few ground rules for a cannabis-friendly Ferragosto:

  1. Don't light up in the crowd. A packed festival street full of families is no place for it, and outdoor dining areas are off-limits under city rules.
  2. If you're doing edibles, do them at home, and early. Start low and go slow; they can take an hour or more to kick in.
  3. Take transit. Driving high is illegal, and parking near a street festival is its own punishment.

For more on what's happening this fall, see our fall 2026 Bronx events calendar.

Know Before You Go

You must be 21+ with valid photo ID to buy. Expect 13% tax at the register (9% state plus 4% local). Adults can carry up to 3 ounces of flower or 24 grams of concentrate in public. City rules ban cannabis smoking and vaping in parks, restaurants, bars and outdoor dining areas. New to legal buying? Read our first-timer's guide and the full where-you-can-smoke rulebook.

The Free Press Take

Arthur Avenue works as a munchies destination for the same reason it has worked as a food destination for generations: everything is walkable, everything travels, and the people behind the counters have been doing this a long time. The two licensed dispensaries on the strip simply make it easier to do the whole evening legally. Buy from a store you can verify, load up on bread and pastry, and bring it all home. Arthur Avenue is for shopping; your apartment is for the rest.

Frequently asked questions

Are there licensed dispensaries on Arthur Avenue?

Yes. State licensing records show Freshly Baked NYC at 2375 Arthur Ave and Guardian Wellness at 2500 Arthur Ave are both licensed and open. You can check any store with the state's Dispensary Location Verification tool.

Can I smoke weed on Arthur Avenue during Ferragosto?

Don't. Cannabis use is banned in restaurants, bars and outdoor dining areas, and a crowded festival street is no place for it. Consume at home.

Which Arthur Avenue food stop stays open latest?

Of the stops in this guide, Ajo y Orégano at 621 Crescent Ave lists the latest published hours, until 10 p.m. Friday through Sunday. Always check before you go.

What does the Friendliness rating mean?

It's our 1-to-5 editorial score weighing vibe, late hours, munchie value, proximity to a licensed dispensary and whether consuming nearby is legal. It is based on public information, not staff visits.
