Bronx Community Board Dispensary Fights: How Siting Works and How to Weigh In

Community boards don't approve dispensaries, but their votes go on the record. Here's how siting works in the Bronx and how residents can be heard.

Key takeaways

  • Applicants must notify the community board 30 to 270 days before filing; the board's opinion is advisory but goes on the record.
  • Dispensaries can't be on the same road within 500 feet of a school or 200 feet of a house of worship.
  • A Feb. 12, 2026 law codified entrance-to-entrance measurement, grandfathering 150+ stores after a measurement mix-up that threatened Allerton's Hush.
  • CB10 backed Victory Dispensary 13-10 in 2025 and a Williamsbridge Road proposal 29-0 in April 2026.

If you want to know where the next Bronx dispensary is going to open, don't just watch the storefronts. Watch the community board agendas.

In the East Bronx, those meetings have become the main public stage for the borough's dispensary debate. And two votes by Community Board 10, which covers neighborhoods including Throggs Neck and Pelham Bay, show how differently the same room can react. In May 2025, the board backed Victory Dispensary on East Tremont Avenue by a razor-thin 13–10. In April 2026, it recommended a proposed store on Williamsbridge Road 29–0.

What does a vote like that actually do? Less than many residents think — and more than many applicants would like. Here's how dispensary siting really works in the Bronx, and how you can get a word in.

How a dispensary gets sited, step by step

Step one: the notice

Under Section 76 of New York's Cannabis Law, anyone applying for an adult-use retail dispensary license has to notify the local government not less than 30 and not more than 270 days before filing. In New York City, the law names the community board with jurisdiction over the address as the body that gets that notice. The state Office of Cannabis Management requires it be delivered by certified mail, overnight delivery with proof of mailing, or in person at the board office.

Step two: the ruler

State law bars retail dispensaries from locating on the same road within 500 feet of a school or within 200 feet of a house of worship. How you measure those feet turned out to matter enormously. In August 2025, OCM issued an advisory that switched to measuring from a dispensary's main entrance to the nearest school property line. That change swept in 108 licensed dispensaries and 44 pending applications statewide.

The Legislature stepped in. On Feb. 12, 2026, Gov. Kathy Hochul signed A10140/S9155, which codified entrance-to-entrance measurement and grandfathered in the more than 150 retailers caught by the mistaken recalculation.

Step three: the opinion

The community board can then "express an opinion for or against" the application. That opinion becomes part of the record the state uses when deciding, and the Cannabis Control Board has to respond in writing explaining how it was considered. But the vote is advisory. It isn't a veto, and it isn't a permit.

The Hush case: when a ruler almost closed a store

For a Bronx example of how high the stakes can get, look at Hush, at 2460 Williamsbridge Road in Allerton. It opened Dec. 14, 2023, as the borough's second legal dispensary, run by cousins Levent and Denis Ozkurt. It sits across from P.S. 89.

When the measurement method changed in August 2025, Hush faced community backlash and the real prospect of closure. The February law was written for exactly this kind of case, and Hush remains on OCM's list of open Bronx dispensaries. The lesson for residents: a store that looks too close to a school may have been approved under rules that have since been clarified. Ask how the distance was measured before assuming a violation.

Victory: a close call

Victory sits one block from Say Less, at 3764 E Tremont Ave, which opened in December 2025. Neighbors who objected this spring, weeks before it opened, raised specific concerns: property values, students who gather at a nearby McDonald's after school, and two licensed shops so close together. Some longtime residents said they were deeply uneasy about the store.

Owners Francisco Gomez and Ali Zindani answered with commitments: armed security during operating hours, ID scanning at the door, 8 a.m. to 11 p.m. hours, 10 to 15 local hires, no on-site consumption, and help for authorities in identifying unlicensed sellers. Those promises are now a public benchmark. We explored what two stores on one block means for shoppers in our Throggs Neck report.

Williamsbridge Road: a unanimous yes

The board's April 16 minutes record the 29–0 vote to recommend I Bud You NY Inc. A unanimous tally tells you the room was comfortable; it doesn't tell you why. We'd caution against reading motives into a number.

A community board vote isn't a veto and it isn't a permit. It's a public record — and the state has to answer it in writing.

How Bronx residents can weigh in

  1. Find your board. The Bronx has 12 community boards. Each posts its meeting calendar and, often, its minutes on nyc.gov.
  2. Watch for the notice. Applicants must notify the board 30 to 270 days before filing, so a dispensary item on an agenda means an application is coming soon or already in motion.
  3. Ask about committees. Find out whether your board takes up licensing items in a committee before the full board votes, and go to both if you can.
  4. Ask concrete questions. Hours, security, ID checks, where customers will line up, local hiring, and how the store's distance from schools and houses of worship was measured.
  5. Put it in writing. Written testimony creates a record the board can cite when it forwards its opinion to the state.
  6. Follow up after opening. Commitments made at a board meeting are worth checking a year later. At its August meeting, the Cannabis Control Board gave licensed stores until their first renewal to deliver community impact plans — the kind of local commitment we examined in our look at where Bronx cannabis tax dollars go.
What a Community Board Can and Can't Do

It can hear from applicants and neighbors, vote to recommend or oppose, and put that opinion in the state's official record. It can't approve or deny a license, move the 500-foot line, or shut down a store. Those powers belong to the state.

The fight that may matter more

Here's what gets lost in the heated meetings: licensed stores check IDs, sell lab-tested products and answer to regulators. Unlicensed shops do none of that, and they never came before a community board at all. By August 2024, the city had closed 199 illegal shops in the Bronx under Operation Padlock — and as our reporting on the Indica Diamond case showed, some padlocked shops allegedly reopen anyway.

If you're worried about a storefront on your block, check it against our map of licensed Bronx dispensaries or the state's license records. If it isn't there, report it through the state's incident reporting page. Enforcement is changing hands at City Hall, too, as we covered in our look at the new sheriff and the future of Operation Padlock.

And if you'd rather see what a licensed store looks like before your own board takes up the next application, there are 23 open across the borough. Buddiez Bronx Cannabis Dispensary, a CAURD-licensed cannabis dispensary in the Bronx on Third Avenue in Melrose that sponsors the Free Press, is one of them.

The next siting fight is probably already on an agenda somewhere in the Bronx. The people who show up will shape it.

Frequently asked questions

Can a Bronx community board block a dispensary?

No. A community board can vote to recommend or oppose an application, and that opinion becomes part of the state's record, but only the state Cannabis Control Board can grant or deny a license.

How close can a dispensary be to a school in New York?

A retail dispensary can't be on the same road within 500 feet of a school or within 200 feet of a house of worship. A 2026 law set the measurement from entrance to entrance.

When do dispensary applicants have to notify the community board?

Under Section 76 of the Cannabis Law, applicants must notify the community board no less than 30 and no more than 270 days before filing their application.

How can I tell if a shop on my block is licensed?

Check the state's Dispensary Location Verification list or OCM license data. If the shop isn't listed, you can report it through OCM's incident reporting page.
