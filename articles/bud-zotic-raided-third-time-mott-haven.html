Bud-Zotic Raided a Third Time: Password Doors, Untested Weed and a Bronx Grow House

Three raids at one Mott Haven address, a hidden grow on Pelham Parkway, and what both tell South Bronx buyers about the unlicensed market.

Key takeaways

  • Bud-Zotic, 516 Timpson Place, was raided May 18, 2026: 500+ pounds from the store, 300+ from a van, two arrests.
  • An August 2025 raid there was valued at $3.2 million; customers needed a password to enter.
  • A closure notice said products were neither tested nor FDA-approved; legal packages carry lab-result QR codes.
  • A May 12 eviction at 750 Pelham Parkway South uncovered a 299-plant grow with illegally rewired power.

Three raids. Same address. Well over 1,500 pounds of product seized in the last two raids alone, nine months apart.

On Monday, May 18, 2026, the Sheriff's Office Joint Compliance Taskforce, working with Homeland Security Investigations, hit Bud-Zotic at 516 Timpson Place for the third time. Deputies pulled more than 500 pounds of marijuana products and THC vape pens from the store and more than 300 pounds of cannabis products from a van. Two people were arrested.

Nearly three months later, the story is worth revisiting, because it's the clearest example in the borough of what the unlicensed market actually looks like once you get past the storefront: repeat business, hidden entrances and product nobody has tested.

The Bud-Zotic file: three raids in two years

August 2025: the password door

The August 2025 raid is the one people remember. The operation had previously been shut down during the city's smoke-shop crackdown, then reopened next door, using an adjacent building for sales while keeping the original location for storage. Getting inside required an elaborate password.

The seizure list read less like a corner store and more like a warehouse: about 1,100 pounds of cannabis flower, 71 pounds of THC vapes, 18 pounds of concentrate, 90 pounds of edibles, plus suspected crystal methamphetamine, an imitation pistol and $21,000 in cash. The Sheriff's Office worked alongside NYPD Bronx Narcotics, the FDNY and the Department of Buildings. Eleven people were arrested.

May 2026: round three

Nine months later, investigators were back. The May raid produced two arrests: a driver operating without a valid license and a person with two open warrants. A closure notice said the products were “neither tested nor approved by the Food and Drug Administration,” and that the business had no license to manufacture cannabis products. The property also sits too close to a school, house of worship or youth facility to qualify for a license in any case.

A password on the door is not a vibe. It is a business telling you, in advance, that it knows what it is doing is illegal.

Why "untested" should worry you

This is the part that matters for the person buying, not the person selling.

Every product on a licensed New York shelf carries the state's universal cannabis symbol and a QR code that links to lab results, often called a certificate of analysis. That lab report is the only way an ordinary buyer can know what's in a jar or cartridge: how much THC, and whether it was screened for contaminants.

At a shop like Bud-Zotic, none of that exists. The closure notice says so directly. That means:

  • You can't verify potency. A gummy labeled one strength might be another.
  • You can't verify what else is in it. No lab, no screening, no paper trail.
  • Vapes are the biggest blind spot. You can't see inside a cartridge, and THC vape pens turned up in this raid, and hundreds of pounds of vapes came out of the Indica Diamond case in Pelham Gardens in June.
  • There's no one to call. A licensed store has a license number, an address on the state list and a regulator. A password door has none of those.

If you're buying for the first time, our guide to buying legal weed in the Bronx shows you how to scan a package and read its lab results.

Meanwhile, on Pelham Parkway: the grow house nobody knew about

Six days before the third Bud-Zotic raid, a very different kind of discovery surfaced across the borough. On May 12, 2026, a city marshal arriving to carry out an eviction at 750 Pelham Parkway South found a five-room hydroponic grow operation with roughly 299 plants. The power had been illegally rewired. Authorities removed about 300 pounds of plants and 147 pounds of flower. No arrests were announced.

The detail to sit with is the wiring. Grow lights, fans and pumps draw serious electricity, and a setup that bypasses a building's electrical system doesn't only put the grower at risk. It puts every neighbor on the other side of the wall at risk of a fire nobody planned for. The Pelham Parkway find came to light only because of an eviction, not a tip or an inspection.

Licensed cultivation in New York happens on regulated farms and facilities run by hundreds of state-licensed growers, and their products are lab-tested before they reach a licensed shelf. Home growing is legal too, within limits: three mature and three immature plants per adult, capped at six and six per household. A five-room commercial grow in an apartment is not that.

Red Flags: Walk Away If You See These

No OCM verification sticker at the door. No ID check. A password, a buzzer code or a back-room entrance. Prices far below licensed stores. Packages without New York's universal symbol or a lab-results QR code. Menus sent over social media or messaging apps. Any one of these means you're not in a licensed store. To report a shop, use the Office of Cannabis Management's online incident form at cannabis.ny.gov/report-an-incident.

What we're watching

  • Whether 516 Timpson Place stays closed. Three raids suggest seals and arrests alone haven't ended the operation. A fourth would say a lot about what isn't working.
  • Who's running the raids. The Joint Compliance Taskforce operates under the Sheriff's Office, which changed leadership this summer. We covered that shift in our report on the new sheriff.
  • More hidden grows. The Pelham Parkway operation was found by accident. If you smell a grow, notice unusual electrical work, or hear constant fans in a residential building, that's a safety issue worth reporting.

Frequently asked questions

Is Bud-Zotic a licensed dispensary?

No. A closure notice said the business had no license to manufacture cannabis products and that its products were not tested. It does not appear on the state's list of licensed retailers.

How many times has Bud-Zotic been raided?

The May 18, 2026 action was the third, following an August 2025 raid valued at $3.2 million and a July 2024 bust tied to a connected Timpson Place property.

What was found at the Pelham Parkway grow house?

A city marshal on an eviction at 750 Pelham Parkway South on May 12, 2026 found a five-room hydroponic grow with about 299 plants and illegally rewired power. About 300 pounds of plants and 147 pounds of flower were removed, and no arrests were announced.

How do I know if cannabis has been lab tested?

Licensed New York products carry the universal cannabis symbol and a QR code linking to lab results. If a package has neither, you can't verify what's in it.
